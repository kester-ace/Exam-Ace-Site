Rules at a glance

Open any rule to review its form, use and signal words.

Simple past

Form: verb + -ed (regular) or the irregular past form. Questions and negatives use did / did not + base verb.

Use: a completed action at a definite time in the past.

Signal words: yesterday, last week, ago, in 2017, when I was a child.

She wrote the essay last night. Did she write it? She did not write it.

Past continuous

Form: was / were + verb-ing.

Use: an action in progress at a specific past moment, or one interrupted by a shorter action (often with while or when).

Signal words: while, when, at 8 p.m. last night, all afternoon.

We were watching the match when the power went out.

Past perfect

Form: had + past participle.

Use: the earlier of two past actions; an action complete before a past point.

Signal words: by the time, before, after, already, never … before.

By the time the bell rang, we had completed the test.

Past perfect continuous

Form: had been + verb-ing.

Use: an ongoing action that lasted up to a point in the past, stressing duration or explaining a past result.

Signal words: for, since, all night, how long.

They had been waiting for three hours when the rain stopped.

Used to and would

Used to + base verb: past habits and past states that are no longer true.

Would + base verb: repeated past actions only, never states.

I used to live in Georgetown. (state) Every Sunday we would go to the beach. (habit)

Past passive

Form: was / were + past participle; had been + past participle; was / were being + past participle.

Use: when the action matters more than who did it.

The Treaty of Concordia was signed in 1648.

Reported speech (backshifting)

After a past reporting verb (said, told, asked), tenses usually move one step back: am → was, finished → had finished, is doing → was doing. Time words shift too: last night → the night before, yesterday → the day before.

“I am tired.” → She said she was tired.

Tense consistency and common errors

Keep verbs in a sequence in the same time frame: He finished his meal and left, not leaves.

Don’t use the present perfect with finished time: say I saw him yesterday, not I have seen him yesterday.

After did / didn’t, use the base verb: I didn’t go, not I didn’t went.

Irregular verbs (166)

Where two forms are given (burnt / burned), both are correct; the -t form is common in British and Caribbean English.

Watch the look-alikes: lie (tell an untruth) is regular: lie – lied – lied. Hang (execute a person) is regular: hang – hanged – hanged. Rise has no object; raise (regular) takes one.
