Communication Studies Assignment: An Expository Essay on Non-Communicable Diseases for Parents

2026-10-13

In this Communication Studies assignment, students write an expository essay that explains non-communicable diseases (NCDs) in children to an audience of parents, using credible evidence and a clear structure.

CAPE Communication Studies

The task

Write an expository essay that explains non-communicable diseases (NCDs) in children to parents and guardians. An expository essay informs and explains. It does not tell a story or argue a side: its job is to make a topic clear, accurate and easy to follow.

ItemRequirement
TopicNon-communicable diseases in children: what they are, how common they are, why they happen, and how families can reduce the risk
AudienceParents and guardians of children aged 5–17. Busy, caring, not medical experts, and easily made to feel blamed
PurposeTo inform: leave parents with a clear understanding and a few realistic actions
Length700–900 words, plus a reference list
FormIntroduction with a thesis, three to five body paragraphs, conclusion. A title and subheadings are allowed
EvidenceAt least five facts or statistics from credible sources, each named in the text
ReferencingA reference list with at least four credible sources, at least two of which you found yourself

Communication Studies focus: you are being assessed on how well you match your audience, purpose, mode and register, not only on what you know about NCDs. Audience: parents. Purpose: to inform. Mode: expository writing. Register: formal but warm, in plain language. You will also justify your choices and evaluate how effective your essay is.

Your essay must explain

  • Definition: what an NCD is, in plain words, and why "non-communicable" matters.

  • Examples and classification: at least four conditions, grouped in a way that helps the reader (for example by body system).

  • Causes and risk factors: what raises a child's chances, separating what families can influence from what they cannot.

  • Prevention: realistic steps for home, school and community.

  • Statistics: facts that show how common NCDs are in children, worldwide and in the Caribbean.

Expository writing rules

  • Use a clear thesis in your introduction that previews your main points.

  • One main idea per paragraph, opened by a topic sentence.

  • Support each point with a fact, a statistic or a brief example, and name the source in the sentence.

  • Use a formal but warm tone, and plain language. Explain any technical term the first time you use it.

  • Do not blame parents. Some NCDs, such as type 1 diabetes and childhood cancers, are not caused by anything a family did.

  • Avoid persuasive pressure ("you must"), personal anecdotes as your main evidence, and unsourced claims.

Thesis statement, main ideas and supporting details

Your essay must have a clear thesis statement, a main idea for every body paragraph, and supporting details for each main idea. When you submit, underline your thesis statement and highlight each topic sentence.

Thesis statement: one or two sentences at the end of your introduction that state the central point of the whole essay and preview the main ideas in the order you will explain them. In an expository essay it is a claim you can support with facts. It is not an opinion, a question, an announcement ("I will talk about…") or a command to the reader.

  • Weak: "Non-communicable diseases are bad for children." (too vague, previews nothing)

  • Weak: "In this essay I will discuss NCDs." (an announcement, not a claim)

  • Strong: "Non-communicable diseases such as asthma, diabetes and obesity are becoming more common in children, but understanding what they are, what causes them and how to reduce the risk helps families take realistic steps to protect their children's health."

Test your main ideas: read only your thesis and your topic sentences in order. They should tell the whole story of your essay in miniature. If a body paragraph has two main ideas, split it. If a topic sentence does not connect back to your thesis, revise one of them.

Justification, evaluation and submission

Justification and evaluation (150–200 words)

After your essay, answer these questions in a short paragraph.

  1. Audience, purpose and register: what choices of content, vocabulary and tone did you make for parents, and why?

  2. Organization: why did you order your paragraphs as you did, and how does the structure help a busy parent follow your explanation?

  3. Effectiveness: how well does your essay inform parents, which source did you trust most, and what one change would improve it?

Submission checklist

  • Title, thesis and an introduction that previews the main points

  • 700–900 words

  • Each body paragraph opens with a topic sentence

  • At least five facts or statistics, each with its source named in the text

  • Reference list with at least four credible sources, two found by me

  • Technical terms explained in plain language

  • No blame placed on parents

  • Conclusion restates the thesis without adding new facts

  • Proofread for spelling, grammar and punctuation

  • Justification and evaluation attached
