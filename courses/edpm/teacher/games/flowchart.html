What happens next?

A flow chart is a diagram that shows the steps of a process in order, using standard symbols joined by arrows. Decisions split the flow into Yes and No branches.

Your SBA portfolio needs either a flow chart or an organisational chart, displayed on A4 landscape paper with narrow margins and a correct layout. Learn the symbols, then chart nine Caribbean office processes.

Mode 1 · Learn

Understanding flow charts

Every flow chart in your SBA uses the same standard symbols. In Microsoft Word they are under Insert › Shapes › Flowchart; hover over a shape to see its name (shown in grey below).

Input or process? Ask: does information or a document come in or go out at this step (a form is received, a receipt is printed, a letter is sent)? Then it is input/output. If someone simply does something (packs, weighs, pays, stamps), it is a process.

Example: lending a book at Vigie Community Library, Castries, St. Lucia.

How to read it

  1. 1Terminator. Every flow chart starts at a Start terminator and finishes at an End terminator.
  2. 2Input/output. The member hands over the book and card: something comes in to the process.
  3. 3Decision. A Yes/No question. Answer it, then follow the branch labelled with your answer.
  4. 4Process. An action: the librarian renews the card. This branch then rejoins the main flow.
  5. 5Yes and No labels. Written beside every flow line that leaves a decision.
  6. 6Flow lines. Arrows show the order. Here the main flow runs top to bottom, with the side branch to the right.
Loops. When a step must be repeated (for example, "offer another time, then check the calendar again"), the flow line goes back to the earlier step. That is the only time an arrow runs against the main direction.

Formatting rules for your EDPM flow chart

  • 1Landscape orientation. Layout › Orientation › Landscape.
  • 2Narrow margins. Layout › Margins › Narrow (1.27 cm / 0.5″ on all sides).
  • 3A title. Centred and bold above the chart, naming the process, e.g. Flow Chart: Processing a Purchase Order.
  • 4Standard symbols. Terminators for Start and End, rectangles for processes, diamonds for decisions, parallelograms for input/output.
  • 5Consistent sizes and font. Every symbol of the same kind is the same size; one font and size throughout.
  • 6One direction. The flow runs top to bottom or left to right, with an arrowhead on every flow line.
  • 7Label every decision branch. Yes and No beside the lines leaving each diamond.
  • 8Tidy lines on one page. Lines do not cross or pass through symbols; the chart fits on one page and is grouped.

Method A · Flowchart shapes in Word

  1. Set landscape and narrow margins first, then type the title.
  2. Insert › Shapes › Flowchart: choose Terminator, Process, Decision or Data. Draw one, format it, then copy it so every symbol of that kind matches.
  3. Right-click a shape › Add Text to type the step inside it.
  4. Join the shapes with Insert › Shapes › Lines › Arrow or Elbow Arrow Connector, snapping to the connection points.
  5. Add Yes and No in small text boxes with no outline, then Shape Format › Align and Group.

Method B · SmartArt for a simple process

  1. Insert › SmartArt › Process, e.g. Basic Process or Vertical Process.
  2. Type one step per bullet in the Text Pane; the arrows are added for you.
  3. SmartArt has no decision branches, so it suits a process without decisions. Change a box with Format › Change Shape if needed.
  4. Most SBA flow charts have at least one decision, so Method A is usually the better choice.

Mode 4 · Which Chart?

Organisational chart or flow chart?

Organisational chart = WHO

  • People and positions, from the top post down
  • Who reports to whom (chain of command)
  • Departments, span of control, line and staff posts

Flow chart = HOW

  • The steps of a process, in order
  • Decisions with Yes and No branches
  • Procedures: what to do first, next and last
