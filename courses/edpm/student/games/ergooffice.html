          What you learned

          • Ergonomics is designing and arranging equipment to fit the people who use it. Well-designed hardware and workstations can be adapted to reduce physical strain and support long, comfortable periods of use.
          • Repetitive strain injury (RSI) comes from extended, uninterrupted, repeated movements such as typing and clicking. Ergonomic keyboards and mice, straight wrists and regular breaks reduce the risk.
          • Carpal tunnel syndrome is a type of RSI: a squeezed nerve in the wrist causes tingling, numbness and pain in the hand. Keep wrists straight and level.
          • Eye strain / Computer Vision Syndrome: tired, dry eyes, blurred vision and headaches. Use anti-glare, flicker-free screens at arm's length, put the screen at right angles to windows and follow the 20-20-20 rule.
          • Posture: feet flat (or on a footrest), knees and elbows about 90°, back supported by lumbar support, top of the screen at or just below eye level. This prevents back and neck pain.
          • Good space vs bad space: eat in a separate break area, organise cables with trays, ties and a surge protector, use in-trays and out-trays with everyday items within reach, add task lighting, keep exits clear, keep the desk professional and the room ventilated, cool and pleasant with plants.
          • Health & safety: tidy cables, never overload sockets, use a surge protector, keep drinks away from computers, keep exits clear and have a first-aid kit and a CO₂ extinguisher for electrical fires.
