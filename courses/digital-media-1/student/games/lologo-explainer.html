From wireframe to prototype: LoloGo

How a food-ordering app for Sint Maarten's roadside lolos went from pencil sketches to grey wireframes to a working, tappable prototype.

Role
UX/UI designer
Tools
Paper, Figma, HTML/CSS
Timeline
6 weeks
Platform
Mobile (iOS, Android, web)

01 · Overview

The problem: great food, long waits

At lunchtime the best roadside lolos in Philipsburg and Cole Bay have queues down the street. People wait 20 to 40 minutes, some give up, and owners juggle phone orders while the grill is full.

How might we let people order from busy roadside lolos without the long wait, while keeping it simple for the owner?

Goal 1 · Skip the queue

Order ahead for pick-up or delivery, and know exactly when food is ready.

Goal 2 · Find the right lolo

See what is open, how far it is and what is on the menu, with prices in US$.

Goal 3 · Help the owner

One simple board for incoming orders, with a switch for sold-out dishes.

03 · User flow

Mapping the journey first

Before drawing any screen, we listed every step a hungry person takes, from opening the app to rating their meal. The main path is the order flow; the owner board is a separate path for lolo owners.

Tap a highlighted screen to see it in the walkthrough. Solid arrows: main ordering path. Dashed arrows: optional paths.

04 · Lo-fi

Pencil sketches: fast, cheap and messy on purpose

Low fidelity means rough. We sketched every key screen on paper in about an hour, with boxes and an X for pictures and squiggles for text. Nobody worries about colour yet, so it is easy to throw ideas away.

Tap a sketch to compare it with the wireframe and the finished screen.

05 · Mid-fi

Grey wireframes: getting the layout exact

Mid fidelity is precise but plain. Every box is placed on an 8-point grid with 16px margins, and the labels are real. Grey only, so the team argues about layout, not colours.

06 · Hi-fi

The mock-up: colour, type and imagery

High fidelity looks like the real thing. Colours come from the island: sea teal, sunshine yellow and grill-flame red on warm sand. Food is shown as friendly illustrations so the app looks the same on every phone.

      08 · Testing and iteration

      Watch, listen, fix, repeat

      We tested the prototype with three people who matched our personas. Each person thought aloud while trying three tasks. We did not help; we just took notes.

      Task 1

      “Order a BBQ chicken plate with rice and peas for delivery.”

      Task 2

      “Find out if Salt Pond Smoke House is open right now.”

      Task 3

      “You own Sunrise Lolo. Accept the new order and mark it ready.”

      What we found and what we changed

      Each change went back into the wireframe first, then the mock-up, then the prototype, and we tested again. That loop is called iteration.

        Checklist for a good prototype

        • Tap targets at least 44 × 44px.
        • Text contrast at least 4.5 : 1.
        • The main button sits low on the screen, in the thumb zone.
        • Every tap gives feedback: a change, a message or a new screen.
        • The same thing looks the same everywhere (consistency).
