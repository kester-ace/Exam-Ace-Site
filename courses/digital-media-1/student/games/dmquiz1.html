Section C — Structured Questions

Answer both parts (a and b) of each question in the space provided.

1. Digital Media Categories. Digital media encompasses three core categories.

a) Define digital media and explain the three core categories it encompasses.

b) Give one real-world example for each of the three categories.

Suggested answer points

(a) Digital media is content that is created, stored, shared and used in digital (binary) form on electronic devices and networks. The notes group it into: digital platforms (the websites, apps and services that deliver content), digitised content (text, audio, video and images in digital form) and digital services (services delivered through digital technology, such as streaming, cloud storage or online booking).

(b) One example of each, for instance: platform: YouTube or a hotel booking app; content: an MP3 of a soca song or a digital photograph; service: a music-streaming subscription or cloud storage.

2. Digital Painting vs Digitized Artwork. Digital art can be created natively or converted from physical originals.

a) Compare and contrast digital painting and digitized artwork.

b) Explain one advantage of digital painting over traditional (physical) painting.

Suggested answer points

(a) Both end up as digital files that can be edited, shared and printed (for example as giclée prints). Digital painting is created directly on a device with a graphics tablet and painting software, so there is no physical original. Digitised artwork begins as a physical painting or drawing that is scanned or photographed, so the original still exists.

(b) For example: undo and layers let the artist experiment without ruining the work; there are no paint or canvas costs; colours are unlimited; and the file can be shared or reproduced instantly.

3. Digital Video Production. Producing digital video involves several distinct stages.

a) List and briefly describe the three stages of digital video production.

b) Explain why non-destructive editing is significant in digital video production.

Suggested answer points

(a) Capture: recording the footage and sound with a camera or phone. Editing: cutting and arranging clips, adding audio, titles, transitions and effects in software. Production (output): rendering, exporting and distributing the finished video in a suitable format.

(b) Non-destructive editing leaves the original footage untouched, so edits can be undone, alternative versions tried and mistakes fixed without re-shooting. This saves time and money and protects the original material.

4. Stop Motion vs 3D Animation. Animation in digital media can be produced through several different techniques.

a) Describe the difference between stop motion animation and 3D animation.

b) Explain how modern stop motion productions often combine traditional and digital techniques.

Suggested answer points

(a) Stop motion photographs real, physical objects (puppets, clay, toys) one frame at a time, moving them slightly between frames. 3D animation builds digital models in CGI software, rigs and animates them on a computer, then renders the frames.

(b) Modern stop motion is shot on digital cameras with frame-capture software (onion skinning shows the previous frame), and is often finished digitally: rigs are removed, green-screen backgrounds and CGI effects are composited, and the film is edited and graded in software.
