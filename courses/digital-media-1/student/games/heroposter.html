The brief: a typographic hero poster

Design a 2:3 portrait poster for someone who inspires you, in the style of a motivational sports quote poster. Your hero could be a Caribbean athlete, artist or community figure, a family member (with their consent), or yourself as your future self.

For screen

1080 × 1620 px, sRGB, PNG (or high-quality JPEG).

For print

A3 at 300 ppi (3508 × 4961 px), CMYK or print PDF, plus 3 mm bleed. A3 is not quite 2:3, so place the 2:3 artwork at 280 × 420 mm and extend the background to fill, or print at 12 × 18 in (3600 × 5400 px), which is a true 2:3.

    Learning objectives

    Linked to Module 3 (Creative Solution Design): design principles, typography, colour psychology and image manipulation.

    By the end of this exercise you should be able to:

    1. Design principles: apply hierarchy, contrast, alignment, negative space, repetition and a clear focal point to a poster layout.
    2. Typography: pair a bold display typeface with a contrasting secondary face (your own handwriting font), and control size, tracking, leading and orientation.
    3. Colour theory and psychology: choose a colour harmony, build a duotone from a tint and a shade, justify the mood it creates for your audience, and check accessible contrast.
    4. Image manipulation: select a subject, apply a gradient map or duotone, add a rim light, fade with a layer mask and add grain, then export for screen and print.
    5. Ethics: use images and quotes legally and honestly, and credit them in APA 7.

    Make your own handwriting font

    Your poster pairs a bold condensed sans with a second face. Make that second face your own handwriting using the free Calligraphr service. You also practise scanning at the right resolution.

    ⬇ Download the template (PDF) Open calligraphr.com ↗

    1. Print the template

      Print the PDF on plain white A4 or Letter paper at 100% / Actual size. Turn off "Fit to page", so the boxes and the four corner markers stay the right size.

    2. Fill it in by hand

      Use a black felt-tip pen (a fine marker, not a ballpoint or pencil). Write one character per box, keeping inside the box, and sit letters on the faint helper line so your baseline is even. Give descenders (g, j, p, q, y) room below the line. Do not draw on or near the four square markers.

    3. Scan or photograph it

      Scanner: 300 to 600 dpi, greyscale (or black and white), saved as PNG, JPEG or PDF.
      Phone: lay the sheet flat in even daylight, hold the phone parallel to the page, avoid shadows and glare, and make sure all four corner markers are in the frame.

    4. Upload and build the font

      Sign in at calligraphr.com (a free account is enough for this template). Go to My Fonts › Upload Template, choose your scan, check each character in the preview and fix or delete any bad ones. Add the characters to your font, then choose Build Font, name it, and download the TTF (or OTF).

    5. Install it, or load it straight into the builder

      Windows: right-click the TTF › Install. macOS: double-click › Install Font (Font Book). Chromebook or school computer: you may not be allowed to install fonts, so load it straight into the Poster builder below. Restart your design app after installing.

    Troubleshooting

    • "Template not recognised": a marker is cut off, covered or in shadow. Rescan with all four markers visible and the page flat.
    • Thin or broken letters: the pen was too fine or pale. Go over them with a felt-tip and rescan.
    • Grey speckles or smudges: scan in greyscale with brighter, even light, or clean the scanner glass.
    • Parts of letters missing: they crossed the box edges. Rewrite them smaller and inside the box.
    • Bouncy baseline: some letters sit too high or low. Use the helper line, or adjust them in Calligraphr's character editor.
    • Blurry edges: rescan at 600 dpi, or photograph in better light with the phone held still.
    • Font not showing in your app: close and reopen the app after installing, or load it into the builder here.

    Lighting the shoot

    The rim light looks most convincing when it was really there. Put one light (or a bright window) behind and to the side of your subject, and expose for the face. Plan it in Lighting Director.

    Deep skin tones: expose for the skin, not the background, and add a reflector or soft fill rather than brightening the skin later. When you cut out, use Select and Mask / Refine Edge on hair so natural hair, locs and braids keep their true shape.

    Use photos you have the right to use

    • Best: your own photo. If it shows family or friends, get their permission in writing with a simple model release (a parent or guardian signs for anyone under 18).
    • Public figures: photos of famous people belong to the photographer or agency and are protected by copyright. Do not take them from Google Images or social media. Use images with a Creative Commons licence (for example on Wikimedia Commons) or a licence you have paid for, and credit them exactly as the licence requires.
    • Check the licence terms: BY means credit the creator; NC means non-commercial only; ND means no derivatives, so you cannot colour-grade or cut it out. SA means share your poster under the same licence.

    Quotes must be real

    • Verify that the quote is real and correctly attributed. Find it in an original interview, speech, book or the person's own verified account, not just on a quote website.
    • Never invent a quote for a real person, and do not change their words. If you shorten it, use an ellipsis (…).
    • For your future self or a hero, write your own words and label them clearly.
    • Cite the source of the quote, and of any photo, in APA 7 on your rationale sheet.

    Don't imply endorsement

    A hero poster celebrates someone; it must not look like an official advert. Do not add team, sponsor or brand logos, and do not suggest the person approved your poster unless they really did. Add a small credit line such as "Student artwork. Not affiliated with or endorsed by the person shown."

    Edit portraits respectfully

    • Light and grade deep skin tones properly: expose for the skin and use a rim or kicker light for separation. Check the face in the duotone; it should be rich and detailed, not grey or muddy.
    • Never lighten skin, slim faces or bodies, or change features. A colour grade changes the mood of the whole image, not who the person is.
    • Leave natural hair, locs and braids as they are. Refine the selection edge instead of smoothing hair away.
    • Temporary blemish removal is fine with consent; permanent features (scars, birthmarks, freckles) stay.
    • If your hero is a real person, show them in a way they would be proud of.
